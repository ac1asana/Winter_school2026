## Project Overview

## Project Overview

Many everyday laboratory observations are still made manually by chemists. They rely on visual judgement to determine whether a substance has dissolved, a material has melted, a reaction has changed colour, a liquid has become cloudy or its flow behaviour has changed. These observations can be subjective, difficult to quantify and challenging to monitor continuously.

At the same time, laboratory experiments present potential safety risks. Containers can overflow, liquids can spill, vessels can be incorrectly positioned, and heating or stirring equipment may be operated under inappropriate conditions.

This project aims to develop an intelligent camera-based vision system that continuously monitors laboratory experiments, tracks visual changes and identifies potential safety hazards in real time.

By combining computer vision techniques with Vision-Language Models (VLMs), we aim to develop a system that can go beyond recognising laboratory objects and provide meaningful descriptions of experimental activity.

## Objective

Develop a vision-based AI system that can observe chemistry experiments, identify important visual changes, monitor liquid flow behaviour and highlight potential laboratory safety issues.

The main objectives are to:

- Recognise laboratory glassware and equipment.
- Monitor visual changes occurring during chemistry experiments.
- Identify dissolution, cloudiness, precipitation and colour changes.
- Analyse liquid flow behaviour and estimate apparent viscosity from video observations.
- Track how experimental changes develop over time.
- Detect potential safety hazards, including spills and overflowing vessels.

## Approach

- **Data Collection:** Record chemistry experiments using a shared laboratory camera.
- **Computer Vision:** Apply image processing, object detection, segmentation and visual tracking.
- **Experimental Analysis:** Investigate changes in colour, appearance, cloudiness, visible solid particles and liquid flow behaviour.
- **Viscosity Monitoring:** Analyse liquid movement, flow rate and deformation to investigate differences in apparent viscosity.
- **Safety Monitoring:** Explore detection of liquid spills, overflow and potentially unsafe equipment arrangements.
- **Vision-Language Models:** Use visual-language capabilities to interpret selected frames and generate descriptions of observed events.

## Experimental Scenarios

The project will focus on the following experimental investigations:

- **Dissolution:** Monitoring salt and sugar dissolving in water and observing changes in visible solid particles.
- **Cloudiness and Precipitation:** Observing the formation of curds and liquid separation when milk is mixed with vinegar.
- **Colour Changes:** Using universal indicator with suitable dilute acidic, neutral and alkaline solutions to monitor changes in colour.
- **Viscosity Monitoring:** Comparing the flow behaviour of liquids with different viscosities by analysing their movement over time.

Alongside these experiments, additional scenarios will be used to investigate laboratory safety monitoring, including spills, overflowing vessels, and glassware recognition.
